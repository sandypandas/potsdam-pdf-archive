When conducting a BCA, a minimum of two project alternatives are considered. A base case, or
no-build, scenario is typically one of the alternatives. A second alternative, the build, assumes
that the project will move forward. Capital costs are estimated for this second alternative, and a
schedule for construction is developed. In addition, the operating and maintenance costs are
estimated, as is their timing. Other variables incorporated into the build alternative for a
highway project include anticipated traffic diversions and changes to the overall levels of traffic
in the area. It is the difference between these alternatives that forms the basis of the BCA.

In general, BCA results in two standard metrics where the streams of future benefits and costs
are discounted to today’s dollars:

e NPV = Discounted Benefits — Discounted Costs
e BCR = Discounted Benefits / Discounted Costs
