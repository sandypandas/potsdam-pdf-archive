The approximately 150-mile-long corridor alignment (see Appendix B) begins with a connection
near Watertown, New York, and ends with a connection near Plattsburgh, New York. The
alignment was designed to minimize impacts on state reforestation lands, state wildlife
management areas, freshwater emerging wetlands, freshwater forested/shrub wetlands,
freshwater ponds, streams, rivers and other bodies of water. The alignment also minimizes the
number of road crossings while maintaining geometry necessary to maintain a 70 mph design
speed. The alignment was designed to provide interchange connections to specific population
areas along the corridor and connections to border crossings.

C. Interchange Connections

There are 13 proposed interchange connections along the corridor that have been strategically
located to provide access to population centers including: Watertown, Philadelphia, Gouverneur,
De Kalb, Canton, Potsdam, Massena, Moira, Malone, Ellenburg and Plattsburgh. The
interchanges are to be diamond-shaped, grade separated and will overpass the existing cross
roads. The design criteria for the interchanges, including cross roads, ramps, acceleration and
deceleration lanes, have been established consistent with Ch. 2.7.5.2 of the NYSDOT HDM and
the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials A Policy on Geometric
Design of Highways and Streets 2011.

Typical of a diamond interchange, the ramps are outer connection-type and retain a minimum
40-mph design speed. They consist of a 15-foot traveled way with a 2 percent cross slope and 3-
foot left and 6-foot right shoulders each with 6 percent cross slopes. The ramp roadside
treatment will be consistent with the mainline roadside treatment. Acceleration/deceleration
lanes are provided at every on and off-ramp connection to the mainline and will be of the parallel
or taper type. They consist of a 12-foot lane and 6-foot shoulder and will .retain the general
characteristics of the mainline and mainline roadside treatment. Connections to the crossroads
are to be stop controlled and aligned to form plain, four-leg, perpendicular intersections. Box
beam guiderail is proposed for the ramps as they will experience elevation change from the
connection of the system to the crossroads. Concrete barrier is proposed for pier protection on
the cross roads where needed.

D. Connections to Border Crossings

The proposed interchange connections discussed in Section III C provide indirect connections to
several Canadian border crossings along the corridor including: Ogdensburg-Prescott
International Bridge, Seaway Bridge, Fort Covington/Dundee Border Crossing, Trout River
Border Crossing, Jamieson Line Border Crossing, Chateauguay/Herdman Border Crossing,
Churubusco/Franklin Border Crossing, Mooers/Hemmingford Border Crossing, and
Champlain/Lacolle Border Crossing.
