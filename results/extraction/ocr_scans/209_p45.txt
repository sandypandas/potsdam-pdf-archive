West Virginia University

In an effort to. better understand the economic effects of interstate highways during their post-
construction period, West Virginia University conducted a study of counties that received
interstate highways built during the 1960s and early 1970s and counties off the interstate system.
A specific focus was on nonmetropolitan cities, the urban fringe, more spatially isolated rural
areas, and nearby off-interstate counties.

The study found that the beneficiaries of interstate links in terms of economic growth are
interstate counties in close proximity to large cities or counties that have some degree of prior
urbanization; for example, a city with more than 25,000 residents. Rural interstate and off-
interstate counties exhibit few positive effects, based on this study.

Dartmouth College and University of Kentucky

A study jointly conducted by Dartmouth College and the University of Kentucky examined the
relationship between large infrastructure spending, of the type implied by interstate highway
construction, and the level of economic activity.‘

retail trade, services, transportation, communications, and public utilities industries. Counties
