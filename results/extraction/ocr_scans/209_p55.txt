VIII. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The potential for a new freeway connecting Watertown and Plattsburgh has been in the public
discourse for a number of years. This report presents a preliminary assessment of the feasibility,
impacts, benefits and costs associated with an approximately 150-mile-long four-lane interstate
highway conceptualized along an alignment roughly parallel to Route 11. The alignment was
conceptualized to provide interchange connections to specific urbanized areas and nearby border
crossings while minimizing environmental impacts. Nonetheless, a new roadway could
potentially intersect or traverse in the vicinity of environmentally-sensitive resources, including
approximately 6,500 acres of NYSDEC wetlands and approximately 7,000 acres of NWI
wetlands, and would therefore be subject to extensive permitting requirements.

The new alignment would be expected to reduce travel times for many trips in the Watertown —
Plattsburgh corridor. It is estimated that travelers crossing much of the corridor could save as
much as 30 to 35 minutes in travel time, possibly more during peak travel times. Short trips
might only be reduced by a few minutes. ‘It is important to recognize that many local trips
(which compose much of the Route 11 corridor traffic) would likely remain on Route 11 and
other local roadways. These trips would benefit from a decrease in through traffic along the
Route 11 corridor, which could result in a decrease in travel time delay. An order-of-magnitude
estimate of potential travel time savings for all vehicles in the corridor ranges from 5 percent to
10 percent. The number of crashes in the corridor would likely be reduced as vehicles are
diverted to an interstate type highway with a lower crash rate.

In order to justify an investment of approximately $1.5 billion to construct a new freeway, it is
important to fully understand the value of its potential benefits. Typically, roadways are built or
expanded to reduce the level of congestion in a region, to minimize longer term maintenance and
operations expenses, or to provide adequate infrastructure to support a growing population or
employment center. In some cases, local economic development is an important driver of
roadway investment, however, the connection between the construction of a rural freeway and
economic development is tenuous. The Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) reflects what $1 of investment
will generate in societal benefits. A project with a BCR greater than 1.0 is considered
economically justified. The BCR for a freeway connecting Watertown and Plattsburgh is lower,
and ranges from 0.4 to 0.8. An investment with this BCR would typically not be advanced.
