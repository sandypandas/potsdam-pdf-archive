Housing

As noted in the demographics section, Potsdam faces several unique housing challenges. Chief among
these is the fact that 70 percent of the Village’s existing housing units are renter-occupied; many of these
units are leased to university students wishing to reside off-campus. Historically, rental housing has
required extraordinary code enforcement efforts to ensure that such units are in decent, safe and sanitary
condition and in compliance with state property maintenance codes.

In 2009, the Village Board of Trustees revamped the Village Code to make it easier to enforce and to
inspect units suspected of violations of the Village law and the property maintenance code. The revised
§134 requires that each landlord provide a floor plan of their units and a parking plan to ensure that each
unit has a sufficient number of parking places per rental unit. Most significant however is that every
landlord must secure a rental housing permit to legally lease units to a tenant. Those units that are found
during an on-site inspection to have serious health, safety and welfare or property maintenance code
‘violations may not be occupied. It is expected that with vigorous code enforcement that the Village’s
housing stock will be continue to provide decent, safe and sanitary housing opportunities for students and
year-round residents of the Village.

‘Property maintenance was cited in committee discussions as being critical to the safety of the Village’s
housing stock, for its long-term economic life, recruiting prospective residents — especially new
employees at the universities and hospital and the general attractiveness of the community as a whole. To
-ensure that property maintenance is a priority for homeowners and landlords the Village will use a multi-
pronged approach that includes:

e Pro-active enforcement of the NYS Property Maintenance Code and Village codes that promote
the quality of life.

e By providing low/moderate income homeowners and landlords leasing to low/moderate income
people with loan and grant assistance for housing rehabilitation.

e By promoting community and neighborhood pride.
