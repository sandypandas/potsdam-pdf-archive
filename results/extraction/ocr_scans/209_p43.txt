VI. ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT ASSESSMENT
A, Introduction

Investment in transportation infrastructure is key to fostering the efficient transportation of
people and goods throughout a region. Because there is a strong emphasis on economic
development at the local and state levels in New York and nationally, considerable interest exists
in improving roadways to make an area more attractive to businesses that might consider
expansion or relocation.

The potential for a new freeway connecting Watertown and Plattsburgh has been in the public
discourse for a number of years. To justify an investment in approximately 150 miles of new
freeway, however, it is important to fully understand the potential benefits of a project of this
scale. Typically, roadways are built or expanded to reduce the level of congestion in a region, to
minimize longer term maintenance and operations expenses, or to provide adequate
infrastructure to support a growing population or employment center. In some cases, local
economic development is an important driver of roadway investment.

Whether a project is justifiable, and what benefits are most relevant in the assessment, is often
based on the perspective of the person or agency making the investment. For example, economic
development may be an important local consideration, but increases in local economic activity
near an interstate or freeway usually represent transfers from other locations and are not truly
“economic development,” when viewed through a statewide or national lens. As a result, any
local economic activity that is generated by the project would not necessarily be relevant when
assessing whether a project generates more benefits than costs from a statewide or national
viewpoint.

The United States Department of Transportation (USDOT) compares public benefits and costs in
determining whether to fund a project through its Transportation Investment Generating
Economic Recovery (TIGER) program. “If it is clear that the benefits do not justify the costs,
the Department will not award a FIGER Discretionary Grant to the project. Benefits include the
extent to which residents of the United States as a whole are made better off as a result of the
project.” Public transportation benefits include reduced emissions, travel time savings, a
reduction in operating costs, and a decrease in accidents, among others.

For this assessment, a discussion related to economic development is provided. In addition, the
benefits and costs of a potential freeway connecting Watertown and Plattsburgh are estimated.

B. Benefits and Costs of Plattsburgh to Watertown Freeway

Frequently, transportation investments are justified based on Benefit-Cost Analysis (BCA),
which compares the value of the savings to travelers resulting from the project investment with
the costs that are incurred during construction and operation of the new transportation facility
(e.g., freeway). This robust approach is well-proven, accepted by USDOT, and is helpful as a
quantitative tool in comparing competing alternative projects.
