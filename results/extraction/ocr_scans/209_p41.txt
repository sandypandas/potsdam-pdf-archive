V. ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENTS

Existing environmental conditions are described in Chapter I, “Existing Conditions,” Section C,
“Environmental,” of this technical memorandum.

The proposed 150-mile long corridor would intersect or traverse in the vicinity of environmental
resources. As noted in Chapter III, “Proposed Corridor Alternative,” the proposed alignment is
designed to minimize impacts on environmental resources, including state reforestation lands,
state wildlife management areas, freshwater emerging wetlands, freshwater forested/shrub
wetlands, freshwater ponds, streams, rivers and other bodies of water. The long length of the
proposed corridor and the environmentally sensitive areas that it would traverse present the -
possibility of significant adverse impacts on environmental resources, and would subject it to an
Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) prepared pursuant to the State Environmental Quality
Review Act (SEQRA) and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA). If selected as a
preferred alternative for further development, the proposed alignment will be classified as a
SEQRA Non-Type II Action pursuant to 17 NYCRR Part 15, and a NEPA Class I action
pursuant to 23 CFR 771.

At this stage of design development, potential affects on environmental resources and
socioeconomic conditions alone cannot be considered a fatal flaw. The potential for impacts on
major environmental resources and constraints is considered below.

A. Noise-Sensitive Land Use

The new alignment would likely be in close proximity to noise-sensitive land uses including

residential land uses, public and community facilities, and publicly accessible recreational areas
(see Figure 4), and would introduce a new source of noise, both during its construction
(primarily due to construction activities) and operation (primarily due to the operation of motor
vehicles). This could potentially result in moderate to significant adverse noise-related impacts.

B. Wetlands

Based on GIS data presented in Figure 6, the proposed alignment would traverse approximately
6,500 acres of NYSDEC wetlands and approximately 7,000 acres of NWI wetlands. Additional
potential jurisdictional wetlands may be located within the new alignment. As noted in Chapter
I, Section C, wetlands measuring 12.4 acres or more are protected under the New York State
Freshwater Wetlands Act, and are under the jurisdiction of NYSDEC. Smaller wetlands may
also be protected if they are considered of unusual local importance. The 100-foot adjacent area
around every wetland is also regulated to provide protection for the wetland. The USACE also
protects wetlands, irrespective of size, under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act. The actual
extent of potential jurisdictional wetlands, and related impacts, can only be determined at a more
advanced stage of design development. Nonetheless, the implementation of the proposed
alignment would be subject to extensive permitting requirements.
