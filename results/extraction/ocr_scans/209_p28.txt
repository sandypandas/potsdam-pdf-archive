TI. PROPOSED CORRIDOR ALTERNATIVE
A, Review of Previous Studies

In 2002, the North Country Transportation Study (NCTS) developed a Final Technical Report,
which evaluated the North Country region’s transportation requirements associated with the
economic development needs of the region. The NCTS confirmed that the lack of accessibility to
the North Country limited the potential economic development in the area. The report concluded
that an east-west highway development approach should be pursued. Also, the approach should
use as much of the existing right-of-way along Route 11 as possible in order to develop a four-
lane expressway, or a freeway standard.

An Action Plan was developed later that year which provided a set of specific actions on both
corridor-wide, long-term projects and specific short-term local projects to improve the North
Country region’s transportation infrastructure and support the economic development needs of
the region.

In 2008, a Final Report of the Northern Tier Expressway (NTE): Route 11 Corridor Study was
developed based upon the recommendation from the NCTS to build a four-lane expressway from
I-81 to I-87 along the Route 11 corridor. Objectives of this study were to increase mobility and
reliability of the surface transportation and improve safety within the corridor, create greater
economic competitiveness for the region, maintain compatibility of environmental requirements,
and enhance the security of the region.

The Final Report included a Corridor Action Plan that integrates and prioritizes the best
performing near-term and long-term improvement recommendations, and provided a framework
for corridor development over the next 20 years.

B. Proposed Interstate Highway Corridor Description

The proposed design criteria for the interstate mainline alternative between Watertown and
Plattsburgh were established consistent with the NYSDOT Highway Design Manual (HDM)
Section 2.7.1 and the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials A
Policy on Geometric Design of Highways and Streets 2011. A typical section (see Appendix A)
for a normal-crowned roadway section was then developed based upon these proposed design
criteria.

The mainline alternative is a four 12-foot-lane divided interstate freeway with a 100-foot-wide,
1:6 sloped grass median. The pavement of each direction is crowned at the edge line shared by
the two travel lanes and sloped 2 percent to both inside and outside edges of the travel lanes. The
inside shoulder is 4 feet wide with a 6 percent cross slope to the median. The outside shoulder is
10 feet wide with a 6 percent cross slope to the outside of the road. A 4-foot-deep ditch with 1:6
backslope and foreslope was introduced on one side of the typical section to illustrate the
roadway sections with flatter cut and fill slopés and sufficient right-of-way. On the other side of
the typical section, the 4 foot-deep ditch has 1:4 backslope and foreslope, which demonstrated
roadway sections with steeper cut and fill slopes and limited right-of-way. Box beam guide
