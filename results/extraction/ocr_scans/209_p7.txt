3. North Country Colleges and Universities

The Route 11 corridor through the North Country connects many institutions of higher education
and learning including: Jefferson Community College (Watertown), SUNY College of
Technology at Canton (Canton), St. Lawrence University (Canton), Clarkson University
(Potsdam), SUNY Potsdam (Potsdam), SUNY Plattsburgh (Plattsburgh), and Clinton County
Community College (Plattsburgh). Combined, these institutions contribute over 25,000 students
to the total population of the North Country.

4. Tourism and Attractions

At the southwest end of the corridor, Sackets Harbor, Alexandria Bay and Cape Vincent in the
Thousand Islands attract visitors to the region. Similarly in the Adirondack region, Tupper Lake,
Lake Placid and Saranac Lake are major draws while on the eastern end of the corridor, the
shores of Lake Champlain provide access to numerous recreational opportunities.

B. Traffic

The travel demands of the study area are attributed to manufacturing, agricultural, commercial,
and institutional land uses of major cities and villages in North Country including Watertown,
Gouverneur, Ogdensburg, Canton, Potsdam, Massena, Malone, and Plattsburgh. Fort Drum, an
Army military reservation, is a major travel demand source in Jefferson County. Major
universities and colleges are also located in the study area, as noted above, and they also are
major contributors to the travel demands of the corridor.

Transportation connectivity in North Country is vital for areas with major United States-Canada
border crossings including Ogdensburg and Massena in St. Lawrence County. The major
highways in the area, as noted previously, provide access to Watertown, Fort Drum, Gouverneur,
Ogdensburg, Canton, Potsdam, Massena, and Malone. This section presents an overview of the
existing conditions along the Watertown-Plattsburgh corridor, including traffic volumes,
origin-destination patterns, travel time and delay, and safety.
