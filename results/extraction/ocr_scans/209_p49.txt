4. Potential Benefits Generated by Plattsburgh to Watertown Freeway

There are a number of benefits to society that may be generated by the new freeway. The
following describes each of these benefits, and the results of the BCA are provided in the
subsequent section. It should be noted that for purposes of this analysis, benefits accrue
incrementally as segments are complete, with full benefits not attained until construction
completion in 2034. The “build” traffic volumes are shifted to the new roadway as construction
is completed.* The useful life of the roadway is assumed to extend 30 years past the full
completion date to 2063.

Travel Time Savings

Travel time savings capture the difference in time spent on the road network in the absence of
the freeway (no-build) to the time spent in the build alternative. Travel time savings reflect
reductions in travel time (VHT) based on reduced congestion and/or increased speeds with
monetization factors (value of time) that vary by trip purpose (personal, trucking, business, etc.).
For this analysis, it is assumed that the value of time is equivalent to $18.90 per hour for an
automobile driver’. It is also assumed that an average of 1.6 people occupy each vehicle. For a
truck driver, the value of time is assumed to be $25.75. The value of time increases by 1.6
percent per year to reflect the real income and productivity growth. These values, combined with
the travel time savings associated with using the new freeway, result in the total travel time
savings generated by the new freeway investment.

Vehicle Operating Costs

Vehicle operating costs are costs that change with vehicle use; for example, fuel, oil, tire wear,
maintenance and repair, and depreciation. Vehicle operating costs may increase or decrease as a
result of a transportation improvement, depending on the difference in vehicle miles traveled and
average travel speed between the build and no-build scenarios. The costs associated with vehicle
operation for both automobiles and trucks were obtained through the HERS Technical Report,
updated with other federal data. Due to the increase in operating speeds, average auto operating
costs increase by $0.02 per mile and average truck operating costs increase by $0.05 per mile.
