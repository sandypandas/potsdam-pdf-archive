Once maximum through flows were established, traffic volume diversion to the proposed
alignment was estimated. To estimate the percentage of diverted traffic from Route 11, the
California diversion curve equation was used. This is a generalized formula that estimates
diverted traffic volumes between two points based on the change in travel distance and travel
time. Estimates were made for the travel distance key points and for the travel time the same key
points. Then new values for travel distance and travel time were estimated based on the
proposed alignment using generalized assumptions about average speed.

General traffic diversion percentages were developed that ranged from 15 percent to 45 percent
depending on the length of the trip and the amount of time that could be saved. For many trips, a
25 percent to 30 percent diversion was likely. The 15 percent diversion values were applied to
traffic on the SR 37 corridor due to the longer travel distance to the proposed alignment. A
relatively conservative value of 40 percent was applied to the Route 11 corridor cut lines as an
initial estimate. The resulting volumes likely overstate the diverted traffic. However, induced
demand in the corridor was not considered in the traffic diversion calculations. Therefore, using
a conservative diversion percentage compensates for the inability to estimate new regional
cross-state traffic that could potentially divert from Interstate 90, Highway 401 in Canada, or
other highways. Table 13and Table 14 present the estimated 2014 No-Build and Build traffic
volumes.

Traffic has not increased substantially in the Route 11 corridor over the last decade and has
decreased in a few locations. Therefore, a nominal growth rate of 0.5 percent was assumed for
background growth in the corridor. A slightly higher growth rate of 1.0 percent was assumed for
truck traffic. Based on these growth rates, the future traffic was estimated for each existing cut
line section as well as each segment of the proposed freeway. The ETC for the freeway is 2034
(20 years from the current year) and the planning horizon is 30 years from completion or 2064
(ETC+30). The projected traffic volumes for these years are presented in Table 13 and Table 14.
The volumes are also presented on Figure 10and Figure 11.

As shown, the volume of traffic on the proposed alignment is estimated to be between 1,400 and
5,500 AADT depending on the location. It is important to note that these are preliminary,
generalized estimates based simply on traffic count data and simplified diversion estimates. If
more detailed information and analysis become available, these estimates may be further refined.
However, the estimated traffic volumes yield a general basis for feasibility level planning.
