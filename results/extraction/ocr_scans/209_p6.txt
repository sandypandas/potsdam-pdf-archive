A. Highway
1. Corridor Characteristics

The 155-mile-long U.S. Route 11 corridor between Watertown and Plattsburgh, New York, is
primarily a rural arterial linking small cities and villages across the North Country within
Jefferson, St. Lawrence, Franklin and Clinton counties. For the majority of its length, the
roadway is two lanes wide. The roadway widens to four lanes within the villages of Gouverneur,
Canton, and Potsdam, and five lanes within the village of Malone to accommodate additional
traffic and turning movements. The small cities and villages along the corridor are the primary
sources of travel demand and include manufacturing centers, employment centers and
educational institutions. Interstate 81 is at the westerly terminus of the corridor.

Interstate 81 is part of the Eisenhower Interstate System. It provides a link between the
Canadian Border and Ontario 401 at its northern terminus at the St. Lawrence Seaway north of
Watertown, New York, and Interstate 40 at its southern terminus near Dandridge, Tennessee.
Combined with Interstate 59 and Interstate 75, Interstate 81 largely follows U.S. 11 for its entire
journey from New Orleans, Louisiana, to Watertown, New York. Bypassing the largest cities of
the northeast, Interstate 81 serves as a link to the mid-southern states and is an important
trucking route. Within central New York State, Interstate 81 is an important north-south
connection between the cities of Watertown, Syracuse (Interstate 90) and Binghamton (Interstate
88 and NY Route 17).

Interstate 87 is at the easterly terminus of the corridor. Interstate 87 is part of the Eisenhower
Interstate System and is the major north-south highway in eastern New York State. It provides a
link between the Canadian border and Quebec Autoroute 15 at its northern terminus in
Champlain, New York, and New York City at its southern terminus with Interstate 278. From
the Canadian border to Albany, New York, Interstate 87 is known as the Adirondack Northway.
South of Albany to the New York City limits, Interstate 87 is known as the New York State
Thruway. Within the New York City limits, Interstate 87 is known as the Major Deegan
Expressway.

2. Major Destinations and Intermediate Villages

On the western portion of the study area, Fort Drum, home of the U.S. Army’s 10th Mountain
Division, is located approximately 6 miles northeast of the City of Watertown. The installation
houses more than 35,000 military personnel and dependent family members. In addition to the
10th Mountain Division, Fort Drum is either the home installation or training installation to a
number of other units from various branches and components of service including Active,
Reserve, Joint, Allied and Interagency (Customs, FBI, state, local emergency services)
organizations. It is the North Country’s largest employer and a major transportation destination.
