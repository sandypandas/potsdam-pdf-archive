B. Travel Times and Traffic Operations

The high-level operational analysis considered two primary elements: travel time and
volume/capacity ratios. The new freeway alignment could be expected to reduce travel times for
many trips in the corridor. It would reduce travel times more for longer trips, with smaller
reductions for shorter trips. It is estimated that travelers crossing much of the corridor could
save as much as 30 to 35 minutes in travel time, possibly more during peak travel times. Short
trips might only be reduced by a few minutes.

Travel time savings will depend greatly on the origin and destination of the trip. For example,
travelers between the east side of Canton and downtown Potsdam would be better served by the
existing Route 11, rather than going northwest to access the new freeway and then traveling into
town from the new interchange outside Potsdam. Thus, it is important to recognize that many
local trips (which compose much of the Route 11 corridor traffic) are likely to remain on Route
11 and other local roadways. However, there will be an overall reduction in travel time for
drivers in the corridor with a new grade-separated facility. In addition, the proposed alignment
could result in a decrease in through traffic along the Route 11 corridor, which could result in a
decrease in travel time delay. An order-of-magnitude estimate of potential travel time savings
for all vehicles in the corridor ranges from 5 percent to 10 percent. ,

The preliminary 2064 daily volume forecasts on the proposed freeway range from 1,800 to
7,100 vehicles per day (vpd). The projected volumes indicate that the proposed highway
alignment would be expected to function at level of service (LOS) A, or free-flow conditions,
throughout 30-year planning horizon. The maximum planning level capacity of a proposed four-
lane freeway is as much as 60,000 vpd. Traffic volumes of approximately 29,000 are consistent
with LOS B, which is near free-flow conditions.

The volumes on the existing two-lane highways in the vicinity of population centers in the
Watertown-Plattsburgh corridor are projected to operate generally in the LOS B to C range
(4,700 to 8,400 vpd) throughout the planning horizon based on rural planning level capacities for
undeveloped areas. However, the LOS for some highway segments could degrade to LOS D,
corresponding to an increase in traffic volume to 14,300 vpd. With the proposed alignment, the
reduced volumes on the existing highways could improve the levels of service on nearly all rural
segments outside of the developed areas to LOS B.
