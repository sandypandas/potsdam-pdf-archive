New York State Complete Streets Bills

Two pieces of legislation in NYS would amend the Highway Law to require bicycle and pedestrian
facilities be included in the planning and development of state, county, and local transportation
facilities, plans, and programs as appropriate.

On June 17, 2010, the NYS Senate overwhelmingly passed its version of a Complete Streets bill
($5711), which would require all roads being built or redesigned using state and federal funding to
consider all users of the road network, including pedestrians, bicyclists, and people with disabilities.
An identical NYS Assembly bill (A08587) has been passed by the transportation committee and is
awaiting a vote in the Ways and Means committee before it can be sent for a full floor vote.

Synopsis of Senate Bill

Provide for the consideration of safe travel by all users of the road network, including motorists,
pedestrians, bicyclists, and public transportation users, regardless of age or ability, through the use of
Complete Street design features for safe travel in the planning, design, construction, reconstruction, and
rehabilitation not including resurfacing, maintenance or pavement recycling of all state, county and local
transportation facilities that are eligible for both federal and state funding and are subject to Department of
Transportation oversight.

Exceptions shall be permissible only after the Commissioner and agency with jurisdiction over the project,
fully demonstrates, with supporting documentation which shall be available to the public, that one of the
following exists:
« Use by bicyclists and pedestrians is prohibited by law; such as within interstate highway
corridors
° The cost would be disproportionate to the need as determined by factors including, but not
limited to, the following: land use context; current and projected traffic volumes including non-
motorized traffic; and population density; or
e Demonstrated lack of need as determined by factors, including, but not limited to, land use,
current and projected traffic volumes, including non-motorized traffic, and population density.
