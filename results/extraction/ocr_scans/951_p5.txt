Daniel Ben Avraham of 8 Circle Drive spoke. He stated that the population of the village is not
growing, taxes are getting higher, and the hospital should look to expand outside the village. He
further stated that today is the start of Rosh Hashanah, there is a large community of Jewish,
Hindu and Muslim people in Potsdam and the Village Board should schedule meetings around
holidays accordingly.

Kathleen O’Rourk of 36 Wavery Street spoke. She stated that she is concerned about the
transition from R-2 to H-1. She stated she purchased her house on Waverly Street two years ago
because she liked the feel of the neighborhood and she would like to see the integrity of the street
kept. She stated that she can see the hospital parking from her house and the parking lot is never
filled and that what is filled is what is closest to the hospital. She further stated that there is
noise from the HVAC system, the lights are bright and using trees as a barrier takes a long time
for them to grow big enough.

Rebecca Weld of 11 Washington Street spoke. She stated that the buffer zone on Waverly Street
should be a continuation of the feel of Wavery Street. She further stated that once you chip away
at an asset it is hard to re-create it.

Shirley Christman of 31 Waverly Street spoke. She stated that her main concern was parking
and that employees of the hospital had been parking on Waverly Street. She asked how many
additional parking spaces would be needed for additional employees.

Lynn Hall of 12 Waverly Street spoke. She stated that she is concerned that the quality of life
will be minimized. She stated that she is concerned about water and sewer issues because in
2010 they had four inches of water in their basement. She stated that she hoped the board would
do their due diligence in regard to those utilities if the expansion happened.

William Pena stated that he does not live in the Village of Potsdam but owns rental properties

here. He stated that knocking down a home changes the looks of the neighborhood. He stated
that a vote on this less than two weeks from now seems a bit rushed.

Gina Hazen of 7 Chestnut Street spoke. She stated that although she understands the vision she
is not sure it fits with the neighborhood. She asked where it is going to end and that it seems like

a run away train. She asked when will the hospital expansion get pushed out to Market Street.

The Mayor then closed the hearing at 6:52 P.M.

Respectfully submitted,

Lori S. Queor
Village Clerk
